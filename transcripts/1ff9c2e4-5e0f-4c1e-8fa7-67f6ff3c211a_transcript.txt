Host: Welcome back to "Tech Talks"! Today, we're joined by Kesaba Chandra Barik, a software engineer with a strong background in AI, ML, and backend systems. Kesaba, thanks for being on the show!
Expert: Thanks for having me! I'm excited to share my experiences and projects with your listeners.
Host: Let's dive right in. You've worked on some really interesting projects, including Listenify, which converts research papers into podcast-style conversations using Large Language Model APIs and Edge-TTS. Can you walk us through how that project came about?
Expert: Sure thing! Listenify was an idea I had to make research papers more accessible. Traditional summaries can be dry and don't always capture the essence of a paper. I wanted to create something that would engage readers and make it easier for them to understand complex research. I used LLM APIs to generate conversational text and Edge-TTS for the voiceovers. It was a great learning experience, and I'm glad to see how well it's been received.
Host: That's really cool. And I noticed you also worked on a project called Fake News Detection, which uses machine learning and NLP to detect fake news articles with high accuracy. Can you tell us more about that project?
Expert: Yes, definitely. Fake news is a huge problem, and traditional fact-checking methods can be time-consuming and labor-intensive. I used machine learning and NLP techniques to train a model that can identify fake news articles with an accuracy of around 95%. It was a challenging project, but the results were promising.
Host: Wow, that's impressive. And I see you've also worked on a job recommendation platform using FastAPI, Angular, and MLflow. Can you walk us through how that platform works?
Expert: The job recommendation platform uses machine learning to deliver personalized job recommendations to users. I used FastAPI for the backend and Angular for the frontend. MLflow is used to track experiments and ensure reproducibility. The platform also uses Docker and GitHub Actions for deployment and testing.
Host: That sounds like a complex but powerful system. And I noticed you're currently working on a project called shradsDB, a persistent key-value store with crash recovery and durability guarantees. Can you tell us more about that project?
Expert: ShradsDB is a key-value store that uses an LSM-tree storage engine and reader-writer lock based concurrency control. The goal is to create a highly available and durable data store that can handle high write volumes. It's still a work in progress, but I'm excited about the potential applications.
Host: That sounds like a challenging but important project. Kesaba, thanks for sharing your experiences and insights with us today. It's been great having you on the show!
Expert: Thanks for having me! It was a pleasure to share my projects and expertise with your listeners.
